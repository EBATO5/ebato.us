Teaching Philosophy

Make learning feel alive.

My classroom philosophy centers on relevance, curiosity, belonging, and the belief that rigorous learning works best when students feel safe enough to wonder out loud.

“All students can learn anything if it feels interesting and relevant to them.”

I prioritize hands-on inquiry because tactile, spatial, and collaborative experiences create more ways for students to connect new ideas to what they already know. Just as important: students should feel welcomed, supported, and respected in the room where we ask them to take intellectual risks.

Featured Practice

Learning by doing.

Three examples of how I turn abstract science into experiences students can manipulate, discuss, test, and remember.

Cells • Microscopy

Microscope Certification Lab

Students choose specimens, predict what they will see, learn microscope parts and safety, pass a “certification” check, rotate through real specimens, sketch observations, and reflect like practicing scientists.

Chemistry • Modeling

Balancing Reactions with Artemis I

An Artemis I launch anchors a two-period investigation into conservation of matter. Students use molecule cards and models to reason about coefficients, atom counts, and why subscripts cannot be changed.

Anatomy • Project-Based

Movement, Muscles & Joint Models

Students connect anatomical vocabulary to real movement through resistance-band demonstrations, then apply that understanding to collaborative joint-model design and assessment criteria.
